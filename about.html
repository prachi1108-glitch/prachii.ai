The story

The first account I ever worked on belonged to a gynaecologist.

Every week I'd go through the questions her patients carried around for months but hesitated to ask. Is this pain normal? Will the pill stop me from having a baby later? My mother-in-law says I shouldn't do this. Is she right?

My job was to turn each of those questions into a post a woman would stop scrolling for, and trust.

That's where I learned what marketing really is. Not trends, and not pretty templates. Understanding someone well enough to say the one sentence they needed to hear.

When I finished school, most people around me went straight into a degree. I made a different call: learn the craft on real brands first, and let the work speak.

Since then I've written for a 50-year-old hospital in Sonipat, designed a brand and a website for a farm-fresh egg business, and grown my own channel to more than 2,300 followers. I also built this website, including the version of me that's been watching your cursor.

I use AI every day, as a thinking partner and as a builder. But the part I care about most is the part it can't do for you: knowing the audience, and deciding what's worth saying.

How I work

  • Audience first. Before I write a post, I write down who it's for and the question they're asking.
  • Every post has a job. Earn trust, answer a question or get an enquiry. If it has no job, it doesn't go out.
  • Speak their language. Literally. Hinglish, when that's how the audience talks at home.
  • Measure, then adjust. What people save, share and ask about decides what I make next.
